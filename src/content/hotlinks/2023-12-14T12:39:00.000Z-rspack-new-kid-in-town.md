Frontend bundling is a task as old as time, and for ages webpack has been its
standard. It's used almost everywhere, including in many well-known tools. But
its main problem is slowness. Today there are worthy competitors —
[Vite](https://vitejs.dev/), [Parcel.js](https://parceljs.org/),
[Rollup.js](https://rollupjs.org/), and others — yet the common pain point is
migrating an existing application from webpack to an alternative. That task can
take an overwhelming amount of time and feel more like a burden than a
pleasure. But what if a fast bundler appeared that was compatible with webpack
configuration?

And one has! Meet [Rspack](https://www.rspack.dev/):

➡️
[https://blog.stackademic.com/rust-port-of-webpack-rspack-the-new-kid-on-the-block-c3a3de569bfb](https://blog.stackademic.com/rust-port-of-webpack-rspack-the-new-kid-on-the-block-c3a3de569bfb)

Sure, being at major version 0 hints at rawness, but the potential is enormous.
Rspack is written in Rust, which gives a huge boost to build speed compared to
the JS-based webpack. A quick search of articles from people migrating from
webpack to Rspack shows there are some catches, but they're solvable. So if
you're looking to jump off the slow webpack onto something more modern, Rspack
could be an excellent option.
