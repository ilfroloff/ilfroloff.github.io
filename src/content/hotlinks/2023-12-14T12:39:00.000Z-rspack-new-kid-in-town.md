The task of frontend bundling is as old as time. Since ancient times, webpack
has been the standard for this. It's used almost everywhere, in many well-known
tools. But its main problem is slowness. Today there are worthy competitors in
the form of [Vite](https://vitejs.dev/), [Parcel.js](https://parceljs.org/),
[Rollup.js](https://rollupjs.org/), etc., but the common problem is migrating an
existing application from webpack to an alternative. This task can take an
overwhelming amount of time and be more of a burden than a pleasure. But what if
a fast bundler compatible with webpack configuration appeared?

And one has been found! Meet [rspack](https://www.rspack.dev/):

➡️
[https://blog.stackademic.com/rust-port-of-webpack-rspack-the-new-kid-on-the-block-c3a3de569bfb](https://blog.stackademic.com/rust-port-of-webpack-rspack-the-new-kid-on-the-block-c3a3de569bfb)

Yes, the major version being 0 hints at rawness, but the potential is enormous.
rspack is written in Rust, which gives a huge boost in build speed compared to
the JS-based webpack. If you quickly google articles from those migrating from
webpack to rspack, there are catches but they're solvable. So if you're looking
to jump off the slow webpack to something more modern, rspack could be an
excellent option.
