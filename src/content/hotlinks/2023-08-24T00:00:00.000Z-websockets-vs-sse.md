Often there's a need to quickly and continuously receive/exchange data with the
server. Usually, WebSockets are used for this type of task. But if your task is
limited to only receiving data from the server without sending data back,
there's a good, undeservedly forgotten alternative - Server-Sent Events. You can
read about the differences, pros/cons here:

➡️
[https://blog.bitsrc.io/websockets-vs-server-sent-events-968659ab0870](https://blog.bitsrc.io/websockets-vs-server-sent-events-968659ab0870)

### My take:

Actually, I started wondering about the convenience of SSE after reading a
longread ([link](https://systemdesign.one/live-comment-system-design/)) about
designing a system for "live comments" (aka comments on live streams). The
design is applicable to any high-load service with live commenting like YouTube,
Twitch, Facebook, etc. The article describes in quite some detail why SSE was
chosen over other alternatives (which are also discussed). It's hard to retell
everything, better read it yourself. But keep in mind that it's not just
WebSockets - there are alternatives.

From my personal experience with WebSockets, I can note two issues:

#### 1. Scaling WebSocket servers

Or rather, the lack of it out of the box. If you don't want to pull in
third-party libraries, you'll only solve the problem by writing your own With
third-party libraries, scaling can be configured (for example,
[socket.io](https://socket.io/) has an
[adapter](https://socket.io/docs/v4/adapter/) for this), but it requires extra
effort from both the developer and the DevOps engineer. By the way, back in the
day the [socket.io](https://socket.io/) MongoDB adapter didn't work correctly
and I ended up writing my own.

#### 2. Incompatibility with proxy servers

Often proxy servers simply don't support WebSockets due to their technical
nature (WebSockets run over TCP, which makes support more complex for proxy
servers). So again, you have to resort to third-party solutions with
long-polling support, like the aforementioned [socket.io](https://socket.io/)
([link](https://socket.io/docs/v4/#http-long-polling-fallback))

So it's worth thinking carefully whether your task really requires setting up
and maintaining WebSockets, or if it can be solved more simply with something
like SSE. By the way, for NestJS it's easy to set up, for example
[here](https://medium.com/trendyol-tech/how-we-used-server-sent-events-sse-to-deliver-real-time-notifications-on-our-backend-ebae41d3b5cb)
