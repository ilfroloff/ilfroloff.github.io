Often you need to receive data from the server, or exchange it, quickly and
continuously — and the usual answer is WebSockets. But if your task only
involves receiving data from the server without sending anything back, there's
a good alternative that is undeservedly forgotten: Server-Sent Events. You can
read about the differences and trade-offs here:

➡️
[https://blog.bitsrc.io/websockets-vs-server-sent-events-968659ab0870](https://blog.bitsrc.io/websockets-vs-server-sent-events-968659ab0870)

### My take:

I started wondering whether SSE was actually convenient after reading a long
read ([link](https://systemdesign.one/live-comment-system-design/)) about
designing a system for "live comments" (i.e., comments on live streams). The
design applies to any high-load service with live commenting, such as YouTube,
Twitch, or Facebook. The article explains in some detail why SSE was chosen
over the other alternatives (which it also discusses). It's hard to summarize
everything here, so you're better off reading it yourself. But keep one thing
in mind: WebSockets aren't the only way — there are alternatives.

From my own experience with WebSockets, I can point to two problems:

#### 1. Scaling WebSocket servers

Or rather, the lack of it out of the box. If you don't want to pull in
third-party libraries, your only option is to write your own 🙃 With
third-party libraries, scaling can be configured ([socket.io](https://socket.io/)
has an [adapter](https://socket.io/docs/v4/adapter/) for this, for example),
but it takes extra effort from both developers and DevOps. By the way, back in
the day the [socket.io](https://socket.io/) MongoDB adapter didn't work
correctly, and I ended up writing my own.

#### 2. Incompatibility with proxy servers

Proxy servers often simply don't support WebSockets because of their technical
nature (WebSockets run over TCP, which makes supporting them harder for
proxies). Once again you have to fall back on third-party solutions with
long-polling support, like the aforementioned [socket.io](https://socket.io/)
([link](https://socket.io/docs/v4/#http-long-polling-fallback))

So it's worth thinking carefully about whether your task really requires
setting up and maintaining WebSockets, or whether something like SSE would
solve it more simply. By the way, in NestJS it's easy to set up — see this
[example](https://medium.com/trendyol-tech/how-we-used-server-sent-events-sse-to-deliver-real-time-notifications-on-our-backend-ebae41d3b5cb)
