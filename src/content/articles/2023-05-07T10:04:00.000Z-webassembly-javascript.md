If you're looking for information about WebAssembly, this article is definitely
for you:

➡️
[https://thenewstack.io/will-javascript-become-the-most-popular-webassembly-language/](https://thenewstack.io/will-javascript-become-the-most-popular-webassembly-language/)

### My take:

The article is excellent and explores the topic less through technical "how-to"
examples (for that, I'd point you to a different article instead:
[https://www.yieldcode.blog/post/native-rust-wasm](https://www.yieldcode.blog/post/native-rust-wasm))
and more through the question of whether WebAssembly is even needed (spoiler:
it is), why JavaScript is the center of its universe, which working tools exist
today, and the vision for the future of WASM as a whole. Inside, you'll find
plenty of links to WebAssembly-related projects, initiatives, and working
libraries. Enjoy the read 📖

As a bonus, Google also has an excellent introductory video on WebAssembly:
[https://youtu.be/RcHER-3gFXI](https://youtu.be/RcHER-3gFXI). It briefly covers
the essentials: what wasm is, where it's used, its portability, and its
performance.

#### Creating WASM modules

Here are a few articles on creating wasm modules:

➡️
[https://medium.com/geekculture/webassembly-for-node-js-13ef6bec0a0](https://medium.com/geekculture/webassembly-for-node-js-13ef6bec0a0)
A walkthrough of a simple module that computes Fibonacci numbers via recursion.
It's more illustrative than practical, but it's great for building a general
understanding of how wasm modules are structured and implemented.

➡️
[https://www.yieldcode.blog/post/supercharge-nodejs-with-rust](https://www.yieldcode.blog/post/supercharge-nodejs-with-rust)
Another Fibonacci module, this time built with the
[Neon](https://neon-bindings.com/) framework. The article is really more about
Neon than about WebAssembly itself, but that doesn't make it any less useful.

➡️
[https://www.alxolr.com/articles/how-to-process-a-csv-file-five-times-faster-in-node-js-with-rust-and-napi-rs](https://www.alxolr.com/articles/how-to-process-a-csv-file-five-times-faster-in-node-js-with-rust-and-napi-rs)
A more practical example you can build on, since it uses the
[napi.rs](https://napi.rs/) framework for creating wasm modules. The task is
reading and processing CSV. Here, as in the previous example, wasm runs several
times faster — which can be critical for this kind of workload.

P.S. Both examples use [Rust](https://www.rust-lang.org/). In my opinion, it's
the most successful replacement for C/C++ (or at least a worthy alternative).
Its complexity is well balanced against its capabilities, and it's a good fit
for just about anything — so knowing it will never hurt, especially now that
major corporations are adopting Rust everywhere.

So if you're considering learning a new language, I'd highly recommend Rust. The
[Let's get Rusty](https://youtube.com/@letsgetrusty) YouTube channel is a great
place to start. The author retells the
[official book](https://doc.rust-lang.org/book/#the-rust-programming-language)
in a lively, engaging way, complete with code examples, and shares a lot of
useful, up-to-date information about Rust.
